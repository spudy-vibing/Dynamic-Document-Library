=== criteria ===
Score this document on two criteria.

## Criterion C1: Accuracy against the data (c1_accuracy, score 1–5)
Check every figure, comparison and factual statement in the prose against the data and the attachments. A figure is accurate only if it appears in the data or attachments, or follows from them by the arithmetic the outline allows. Quote up to three passages that support your score; list every inaccurate statement under "issues".
5 — Every figure and factual statement matches the data or attachments; comparisons point the right way; nothing is invented, attributed to the wrong metric or period, or stated where the data has no value.
4 — No error a reader would act on; at most one imprecise but not wrong statement (loose rounding, a vague "about").
3 — One minor factual slip (a figure off by rounding beyond the data's precision, a secondary figure the data does not give) that does not change any conclusion.
2 — One clear factual error: a figure that is not in the data, a figure attached to the wrong metric, team or period, a comparison in the wrong direction, or a value stated for a metric the data marks as missing.
1 — Several clear factual errors, or one error in a headline figure or conclusion that a reader would act on wrongly.

## Criterion C2: Coherence across sections (c2_coherence, score 1–5)
Read the whole document. Quote up to three passages that support your score; list contradictions and inconsistencies under "issues".
5 — Reads as written by one author in one sitting: a clear through-line, sections refer to each other where useful, terms and figures consistent throughout, no contradictions.
4 — One through-line; at most one weak transition or one minor term inconsistency.
3 — Sections are individually fine but read as adjacent essays: little cross-reference, abrupt shifts, OR one noticeable inconsistency in terms/emphasis.
2 — Noticeable contradiction between sections (fact, recommendation or tone), OR the document has no discernible core message.
1 — Sections conflict on facts or conclusions in ways a reader would act on wrongly.
